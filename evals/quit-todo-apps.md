# I quit to-do apps and doubled my productivity

For years I kept my life inside to-do apps. I tried Todoist, Things, TickTick, and for a long stretch a notebook with a colour-coded pen system. Every Sunday evening I would sit down, sort the backlog, drag tasks into the week, and feel productive. Then Monday arrived and I would spend the first hour rearranging the list instead of doing anything on it.

Two weeks ago I deleted every app from my phone and laptop. No export, no archive, nothing. I just closed the last one and went to make coffee.

The change was immediate. Mornings stopped starting with a wall of red overdue badges. I sat down, thought about what mattered, and did it. By the end of the first week I was finishing things I had been carrying around for months. The guilt that used to follow me around, the feeling that some small forgotten task was about to blow up, simply faded.

I now get through roughly twice as much real work as before. Quitting to-do apps doubled my productivity, and I honestly believe it can do the same for you.

What I realised is that a list is a way of postponing decisions. Every item you add is a small promise that future you will deal with it, and future you is already tired. Without the list, the only tasks that survive are the ones that actually matter, because you remember them.

If you feel buried by your task manager, try deleting it today. Do not wait for the perfect moment or a quiet week. Close the app, make a coffee, and see what is still on your mind when you sit back down. That is your real to-do list.
